## Building multi-agent swarming environment 

Major part of my research activities at WUT AI unit is to explore multi-agent and swarming environments. Me and my team aim to focus to build small models tailored to work in groups, reason and find cooperative solutions to the problems.

Task we are taking is bit bigger, as it involves not only training models, but also building technology foundations for our work. That is as far as we are considered important to crave enough room, to build control and independence around technology stack we are using, giving us flexibility we need in tailoring it to experiments we will be conducting.

To be bit more detailed our first step we are taking is to build robotic environment. Including simulator based on physics engine, allowing us to test and train models in environments of our design.    

Our goal is to create an implementation that allows us to develop algorithms in simulation and then test them in the physical world, bridging the [simulation-to-reality gap](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212827122007004). Additionally, we want a robotic platform that could be modeled at varying levels of precision. Ideally, it should support both a simple point-in-space representation and behavior that translates well to the real world—a perfect starting point.

Naturally, drones such as quadcopters or n-copters became the ideal platform for us, being very easily represented in digital worlds, letting us to focus on training their models with different degrees of body control precision, and then making physical world verification very feasible.

Body of the drone as mentioned can be controlled to different degrees of precision, ranging from a point in space controlled by a thrust vector to a full physics-based model with separate force vectors for each motor. Since a drone is essentially a rigid body that can also be thought of as a “floating sphere/point” in the physical world, it provides an excellent foundation for our research.

Additionally it is extremely easy to buy multiple drones in batches, and currently technology as a robotic platform seems to be mature enough to allow for different experimentation for us as researchers. 

## Space we carve out (a.k.a middleware we sit in)

As responsible AI researchers we want to stay compatible with standards, so our work is reproducible. That turns us towards designing simulator and environments compatible with very popularly used RL framework: gymnasium. 

It is not yet physics engine we design, and it is more than just gymnasium environment. Our goal is to deliver universal environment template for multi-agent drones environments, giving us tools to train and research swarms and their ability to cooperate or solve problems.

Hence right now we are building middleware tools allowing to run and manage all RL related pipelines like:
- distributing environment observations between agents,
- maintaining multiple agent controllers,
- detect events,
- distributing rewards to agents

Additionally we want to give tool to easily design scenes and tasks. This is majorly important for collaborations, letting other research groups build their own experiments based on our tooling.

<img src="../assets/Untitled-2025-05-07-1014 1.png" alt="Boids" width="700"/>

Everything here is built with two assumptions:

1. **Compatibility with RL Gymnasium** - for instance similar to [EvolutionGym](https://evolutiongym.github.io/)
2. **Flexibility and extensibility** - to easily support new drone models, allow modifications, and make the platform reusable for other research groups.

As on diagram above, there are few certain blocks we are working on:
- Drone Modeling - focusing on modelling physical parameters of drone,
- Scene Manager - maintaining and managing all scene objects, their states, and observations,
- Agent Controller - learning and controlling drone agents,
- Simulator Environment - everything using physics engine and doing physics engine related computations,
- Environment editor - editor helping to design own tasks and experiments
